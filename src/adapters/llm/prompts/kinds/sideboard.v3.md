## A sideboard

A sideboard («aparador», «credenza» or «trinchador») is a low cabinet for the dining room: doors and drawers below, often a row of small drawers or open niches above, on legs or a kick plate.

- "Three doors" is three leaves, one per opening, unless the person asks for double doors.
- Count the columns from what sits side by side, not from how the request lists it: three doors and a drawer column below, under a small drawer and three niches, is four columns, not five.
- The upper row does not have to line up with the lower one: each column stacks its own openings with its own heights, and a niche can sit on top of the drawer column.
- Keep the heavy part low (doors and drawers below, open above): it lowers the center of gravity.
- Low is not the same as safe: at its usual height it passes {{storageAnchorHeight}} with doors and drawers, so `wallMounted` is true unless the person says it will not go against a wall.
